01 — The basicsWhat kind of matters does Beenleigh hear?

Beenleigh Magistrates Court is one of the busier suburban Magistrates Courts in South-East Queensland, sitting on the southern edge of Logan. It hears summary criminal matters from across the Beenleigh, Eagleby, Mount Warren Park, Yarrabilba and Jimboomba catchments — including drink driving, drug driving, simple drug possession, common assault, public nuisance, breach of bail, and DV applications.

It also acts as a committal court for indictable matters destined for the Beenleigh District Court (which sits in the same building) and conducts bail applications most weekday mornings.

i

Watchhouse arrests

If you've been arrested overnight, you'll be brought up from the Beenleigh Watchhouse for a first appearance from the dock. Family can sit in the public gallery — but you won't be able to speak to them before the matter is called.

02 — Getting thereAddress, parking & public transport

The courthouse sits on James Street, two blocks from Beenleigh railway station. If you can take the train, take the train.

  • Train: Beenleigh line, alight at Beenleigh Station (4-minute walk).
  • Driving from Brisbane: M1 south to Exit 35 (Beenleigh), follow City Road to James Street.
  • Driving from Gold Coast: M1 north to Exit 35.
  • Parking — Beenleigh Town Square: Free 2hr underground parking, 4-minute walk to court.
  • Parking — Council car park: Free 3hr at the rear of Beenleigh Library.
  • Avoid: James Street kerbside meters — heavily patrolled, 1-hour limit.

"Three quarters of clients who run late say they couldn't find parking. Park at Town Square. Walk five minutes. Be early."

03 — On the dayWhat time do I arrive, and where do I go?

Court opens at 8:30am and lists are called from 9:00am sharp. Aim to be inside by 8:30 — you'll need to clear security screening (similar to airport: bag through scanner, walk through metal detector) and find your courtroom.

!

If you're running late

Phone the registry on (07) 3804 4555 and tell them. Don't just turn up at 9:30 hoping no one noticed — a warrant for your arrest can be issued the moment your name is called and you're absent.

04 — Inside the courtroomWhat happens when your name is called

The bench announcer will call out the daily list at 9:00am to confirm everyone is present. You stand briefly when your name is read. After that, the Magistrate works through matters in roughly this order:

  • Mentions and adjournments (quick procedural matters)
  • Pleas of guilty (sentence on the day)
  • Bail applications
  • Defended hearings (rare on a busy mention list)

When your matter is called, walk to the bar table, stay standing, and your lawyer (or you) will speak. The Magistrate will tell you when to sit, when to stand, and when you're done.

Forms of address

The Magistrate is "Your Honour". The police prosecutor is "Sergeant" or "Senior Sergeant". The court orderly will direct you where to stand. Don't interrupt — wait until you're invited to speak.

05 — Practical thingsPhones, food, smoking & your support people

  • Phones: Silent or off in court. Photography is prohibited in the courthouse.
  • Food: No cafe inside the building. Beenleigh Town Square has cafes 4 minutes' walk away. Bring water — security allows sealed bottles.
  • Smoking: No smoking within 5 metres of the court entrance.
  • Children: Permitted in the public gallery. Discouraged for serious sentences. There are no childcare facilities.
  • Support people: Family and friends can sit in the public gallery. They cannot sit at the bar table or speak on your behalf.
  • Interpreters: Free, but must be requested in advance via the registry.

The courtroom

How the room is laid out.

Knowing where everyone sits removes a layer of stress on the day. Beenleigh's criminal courtrooms follow the standard Queensland Magistrates Court layout — Magistrate at the front, prosecutor and defence at the bar table, public gallery at the back.

Magistrate's bench

Raised. The Magistrate sits at the front and faces the room.

Bar table

Prosecutor (police) on one side, your lawyer on the other.

Dock

For people in custody only. If you're on bail, you stay in the gallery until called.

Witness box

For witnesses to give evidence. Empty on most mention days.

Public gallery

Where you and your support people sit until your matter is called.

A morning at Beenleigh

What your day will actually look like.

Hour by hour — assuming you're listed for a 9:00am mention or plea on a typical Tuesday.

8:00 am

Park at Town Square

Free 2-hour underground parking. Short walk down James Street to the courthouse. Coffee from any of three cafes on the way if your nerves can handle it.

8:30 am

Through the door, through security

Bag through scanner, walk through metal detector. Belt off if it sets it off. Phone goes through too — stays with you, just on silent inside court.

8:40 am

Find your name on the list

Daily lists are posted on noticeboards in the foyer. Find your name, note your courtroom (1, 2 or 3) and your matter number.

8:50 am

Brief chat with your lawyer

Outside the courtroom. Your lawyer confirms the plea, runs through likely outcomes, and approaches the prosecutor for last-minute negotiations.

9:00 am

"All rise"

Magistrate enters. Bow when they bow. Sit when they sit. The bench announcer reads the list — stand briefly when your name is called.

9:00 – 11:30

The wait

Matters are called in roughly this order: mentions, pleas, bail apps, defended hearings. You wait in the public gallery — sometimes 15 minutes, sometimes 90.

When called

You stand at the bar table

Walk to the bar table. Your lawyer speaks. Magistrate may ask you a direct question — answer honestly, briefly, and address them as "Your Honour".

After

Result & paperwork

Magistrate hands down sentence or adjourns. Court orderly hands you any paperwork. Your lawyer walks you out, debriefs in the foyer or back at Town Square. Done by lunch, usually.

What to wear

Smart casual minimum.

Magistrates have heard every excuse. They've also seen every outfit. What you wear is a small but real signal of how seriously you're taking the matter — and Magistrates notice.

Do

  • Closed shoesBoots, sneakers, loafers — anything that covers the toes.
  • Collared shirt or blousePolo, button-up, modest top — neutral tones preferred.
  • Long trousers or a skirt below the kneeChinos, jeans (clean, no rips), tailored pants, knee-length skirt.
  • Cover visible tattoos if practicalLong sleeves or a jacket. Not mandatory, but it helps.
  • Tidy hairTied back if long. Comb it before you walk in.

Don't

  • Hats or sunglasses indoorsRemoved at the door. The orderly will tell you. Don't make them.
  • Singlets, crop tops, low-cut topsNo exposed shoulders or stomachs in court.
  • Thongs, slides, or open-toe shoesNot even on a 38°C summer day. Closed shoes only.
  • Shorts, gym wear, ripped jeansNo exceptions. The Magistrate will adjourn rather than hear you.
  • Logos, slogans or political messagingPlain shirts only. Brand logos are fine; messages are not.
